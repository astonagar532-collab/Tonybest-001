Itineraries: 48 hours to 7 days

A generic first-visit shape you can adapt to any city. It is built around one paid highlight a day, walking instead of driving, and a rest afternoon so you are still enjoying day four.

48 hours

The default arrival plan. Two days is not enough for a region with long transfer times — check how long it takes to get there first.

    Building 3, 5 and 7 day versions

    1. Day 1 — orient. Walk, eat, buy a transit card. Nothing paid beyond your first night.
    2. Day 2 — one big thing. The attraction you would regret missing, booked in advance.
    3. Day 3 — free day. Market, park, neighbourhood of interest, one long meal. This is where trips go wrong when it is skipped.
    4. Day 4 — day trip. One region, one village, one trail. Cheaper transport, less planning.
    5. Day 5 — slow repeat. Revisit the best area of day 2 at a different hour. Everything is cheaper and emptier off-peak.
    6. Day 6 — buffer. Weather, a delayed train, a museum you missed, or a day trip you did not expect to enjoy.
    7. Day 7 — last morning. Breakfast nearby, checkout rules checked, airport or station transfer timed for the worst case.

    For multi-city trips, spend your buffer days on the city you are least likely to return to, not the one you liked most.

    How to keep days cheap

    • Group everything you want to see by neighbourhood, then walk it.
    • One paid highlight a day is a good rule; two rarely improves the day.
    • Keep one free evening a week for markets and street food.
    • Move between cities early in the morning or late in the evening when fares are lower.
    • Alternate intensity: heavy day, then deliberately easy day.

    Quick checklist

    • Group sights by area
    • Pre-book only what sells out
    • Add a rest afternoon every three or four days
    • Check public holidays and local school holidays before you travel
    • Leave one unscheduled day in longer trips

    How to book this

    1. Shortlist 2–3 options with free cancellation.
    2. Check area noise, late check-in and luggage rules.
    3. Compare refundable vs. non-refundable totals, including fees.
    4. Save the confirmation offline and set a cancellation reminder.
