Things to do: free first, then paid highlights

Build a day out of what is free, then pay for one thing that only that place can offer. It costs less and feels better than queueing for four attractions.

Free and low-cost, in any city

  • A self-guided walking loop between the old quarter, the market and the river or waterfront
  • Public parks, gardens, waterfront promenades and free viewpoints
  • Municipal museums with free or reduced entry days
  • Markets and street markets, which are free to enter even if the food is not
  • Places of worship with notable interiors, where entry is free and dress is modest
  • A neighbourhood food or walking tour run by locals rather than a global platform
  • Sunset or sunrise from a hill, bridge or rooftop bar you were going to eat at anyway

Check the city tourism office or the national museum site for free-entry calendars before you travel — they change by season.

When a paid highlight is worth it

Pay for something that is genuinely unique to this place and hard to substitute: a guided interior, a summit viewpoint, a workshop, or a national park permit. Do not pay twice for the same view.

Quick checklist

  • Group sights by area so you are not crossing the city twice
  • Pre-book only what sells out, or what needs a fixed date
  • Reserve timed entries for weekends and peak season
  • Keep a rain or heat backup plan that is indoors and free
  • Check dress rules and photography limits before you visit a site

How to book this

  1. Shortlist 2–3 options with free cancellation.
  2. Check area noise, late check-in and luggage rules.
  3. Compare refundable vs. non-refundable totals, including fees.
  4. Save the confirmation offline and set a cancellation reminder.
