Accessibility notes

Accessibility varies enormously between destinations, and often between neighbourhoods in the same city. Plan for the weakest link in your route — usually the station, the airport or an old building — rather than assuming the whole trip works.

Before you book

  • Contact the operator directly about step-free access, lift reliability and boarding assistance.
  • Ask an accommodation provider about step-free entrances, bathroom dimensions and bed height — not just “accessible room”.
  • Check whether the attraction has an accessible route, and whether a staff member must accompany you.
  • Plan for longer transfer times: accessible routes often add 10–20 minutes per journey.
  • Book assistance 48–72 hours ahead where required, and confirm by email.

Public transport

  • Most metro and major bus interchanges have step-free access and priority seating — verify lift status on the operator’s site before travel.
  • Low-floor buses and trams are common but not universal; some routes only use high-floor vehicles.
  • Tactile paving and audible announcements vary widely. Expect them in some cities and not at all in others.
  • Taxis and ride-hailing are the most reliable fallback; request a wheelchair-accessible vehicle in advance where possible.
  • Keep power banks to hand — many stations have long walks and few places to charge.

Attractions and heritage sites

  • Old towns often have cobbles, stairs and narrow lanes with no step-free alternative — ask for the specific accessible entrance.
  • Ask about quiet rooms, sensory-friendly hours, or lower-capacity visiting times.
  • Check step counts, lift dimensions and the maximum door width for anything you need to get through.
  • Reserve wheelchairs or mobility scooters where they are offered, and do it early in peak season.
  • Carry a printable version of your itinerary; signal reliability varies.

Sensory and communication

  • Carry earplugs or a noise-cancelling headset — the best-planned days are still loud in many cities.
  • Request a quiet room away from lifts, corridors and any club or bar in the building.
  • Pack a card with key phrases in the local language, plus your allergy or medical needs.
  • Ask hotels and restaurants about staff training; it varies more than you would expect.
  • Use the site’s own accessibility notes and operator information rather than relying on traveller reviews alone.

Quick checklist

  • Call ahead and confirm assistance is actually arranged
  • Plan shorter days with real rest breaks
  • Carry a printed plan and your confirmation references
  • Allow extra time at every transfer point

How to book this

  1. Shortlist 2–3 options with free cancellation.
  2. Check area noise, late check-in and luggage rules.
  3. Compare refundable vs. non-refundable totals, including fees.
  4. Save the confirmation offline and set a cancellation reminder.
