Food and markets

Eating well is usually the cheapest part of travelling well. The trick is knowing where the food is cooked for locals rather than for visitors.

What a cheap day of eating looks like

  • Breakfast: a cafe or bakery near your area, not the hotel restaurant
  • Lunch: a daily set menu, which is usually cheaper and faster than ordering a la carte
  • Dinner: street stalls or a night market, or a late set menu at a family-run place
  • Water: tap where it is safe to drink, or refill stations — carry a bottle

One restaurant meal a day is a reasonable budget. Spending it on the place you have waited longest for tends to be worth more than three mediocre meals.

    Simple safety checks

    • Busy stalls turn food over faster — high turnover is a good sign
    • Prefer food cooked in front of you, and eaten while it is still hot
    • Choose water or soft drinks rather than unsealed ice if you have just arrived
    • Check tap-water advice from your government’s travel health site for your destination
    • Ask about ingredients if you have allergies — language barriers cause cross-contamination more often than you would think

    Quick checklist

    • Check which days the market you want is actually open
    • Carry a refillable bottle and small notes for stalls
    • Ask about vegetarian, vegan and allergy ingredients if relevant
    • Keep an emergency food supply for long journeys between towns

    2. Check area noise, late check-in and luggage rules.
