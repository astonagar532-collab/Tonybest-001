Getting around: airports, fares and late nights

Transport is usually the single biggest controllable cost in a trip. Compare total door-to-door time, not just the ticket price.

From the airport

Typical patterns rather than fixed prices. Check the official operator or airport site for your destination — a handful of cities have no rail link at all.

Local public transport

  • Tap in and out every time. Forgetting a tap is the most common fine travellers get.
  • Buy the right product: single fare, multi-trip, daily or weekly cap. A weekly pass often beats four daily tickets.
  • Check off-peak discounts and whether they exclude weekends or public holidays.
  • Learn the last-service time for your line, not just for the network.
  • Keep the same payment method you loaded, or the fare gate may charge a penalty.

Common city routes

Illustrative examples of the kind of short hop you will do most days. Use them to sanity-check a daily travel budget, not as a real timetable.

When public transport stops

Many cities have no transit service between roughly midnight and 05:00. Decide your fallback before the first night:

  • Save the official taxi or licensed ride-hail number, and the app that works locally.
  • Agree or check the price before you set off; never follow an unlicensed driver.
  • Group the fare with your travel companions before you go in.
  • Plan the first connection of the day in reverse — early trains run less often than late ones.

Quick checklist

  • Plan and price your late-night ride backup
  • Download the official transit app or a PDF map before you land
  • Keep a card and small change ready for machines that reject contactless
  • Photograph every ticket and pass — inspections are frequent

How to book this

  1. Shortlist 2–3 options with free cancellation.
  2. Check area noise, late check-in and luggage rules.
  3. Compare refundable vs. non-refundable totals, including fees.
  4. Save the confirmation offline and set a cancellation reminder.
